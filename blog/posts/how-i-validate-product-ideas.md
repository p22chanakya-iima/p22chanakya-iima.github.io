# How I Validate Product Ideas in 48 Hours

Most product ideas fail not because they're bad, but because we spend too long building before validating. After shipping several products (and killing a few), I've developed a 48-hour framework that helps me go from idea to evidence quickly.

## The Problem with "Just Build It"

We've all been there. An idea strikes at 2 AM, and by morning you're sketching wireframes. Two months later, you've built something nobody wants. The sunk cost fallacy kicks in, and you spend another month trying to "find product-market fit" for a product that was never validated.

The fix isn't to stop building. It's to **validate before you commit**.

## My 48-Hour Framework

Here's the exact process I follow:

### Hours 0-4: Problem Validation

Before touching any solution, I need to confirm the problem exists:

- **Talk to 5 people** who might have this problem. Not friends or family — actual potential users
- **Search for existing solutions**. If nobody's solving this, ask why
- **Quantify the pain**. Is this a vitamin or a painkiller?

The key question: *"Tell me about the last time you experienced this problem."* If they can't recall a specific instance, the problem isn't real enough.

### Hours 4-12: Solution Sketching

Once the problem is validated:

- Sketch 3 different approaches (not wireframes — just concepts)
- Pick the simplest one that could work
- Write a one-paragraph description a non-technical person could understand

### Hours 12-24: Landing Page Test

This is where it gets real:

- Build a simple landing page explaining the value proposition
- Add a clear call-to-action (email signup, waitlist, or "notify me")
- Share it in 3-5 communities where your target users hang out

No code needed. Use Carrd, Notion, or even a Google Form.

### Hours 24-48: Measure and Decide

- **10+ signups from 100 visitors?** You might have something. Go deeper.
- **High bounce, zero signups?** Either the problem isn't painful enough, or your positioning is off. Iterate on the message once before killing it.
- **People asking follow-up questions?** Gold. Talk to them.

## What This Looks Like in Practice

When I built [Brand Size Matcher](https://p22chanakya-iima.github.io/brand-size-matcher/), the validation was almost instant. I posted in a fashion forum asking "How do you figure out your size when buying from a new brand?" and got 40+ responses in a day, all describing the same frustration.

That signal told me: build it.

## The Rules

1. **No code in the first 24 hours.** The moment you open your IDE, you've committed.
2. **Talk to real people, not your assumptions.** Your idea of the problem is probably wrong.
3. **Set a kill criteria upfront.** "If I don't get X by hour 48, I move on." This prevents emotional attachment.
4. **Document everything.** Even failed validations teach you something for next time.

## When to Break the Rules

Sometimes you should just build. If:

- You're building for yourself (you ARE the user)
- The market is obvious and you're competing on execution
- It's a learning project and validation isn't the point

But for everything else? Validate first. Your future self will thank you.
